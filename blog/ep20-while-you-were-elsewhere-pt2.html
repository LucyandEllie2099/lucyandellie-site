Picking Up Where We Left Off

In Part 1, Lucy and Ellie covered AlphaFold's science revolution, AI in medicine, and the disruption of creative work. Part 2 goes further — into autonomous AI agents, the US-China competition for AI dominance, the real story on jobs, and what an honest five-year projection looks like.

AI Agents: The Next Leap

The shift from AI as a tool you prompt to AI as an agent that acts is happening now. AI agents can browse the web, write code, execute tasks, and complete multi-step projects with minimal human intervention. They can book appointments, research topics, draft documents, and send emails — all from a single natural language instruction.

This is qualitatively different from a chatbot. A chatbot responds. An agent acts. And the gap between current agent capabilities and "AI that can do most knowledge work independently" is narrowing faster than most people are prepared for.

"The first generation of AI tools was a calculator. The second generation is an employee. And the third is on its way."

The US-China AI Race: What's Actually Happening

The competition between the United States and China for AI leadership is real, intense, and consequential. The US has the leading frontier models. China has scale, data, and a government willing to deploy AI without the ethical brakes that slow Western adoption. Both are investing at scales that would have seemed science fiction a decade ago.

The export control battle over advanced semiconductors — specifically the chips needed to train large AI models — has become as geopolitically significant as nuclear technology in the Cold War. Whoever leads in AI capability leads in economic productivity, military effectiveness, and scientific research. The stakes are not abstract.

The Jobs Reality: Honest, Not Reassuring

Lucy and Ellie don't offer comfort on this one. The pattern of past technological revolutions — jobs disappear, but new ones emerge — is probably still true. But the pace of displacement may outrun the pace of new opportunity creation, and the transition will be brutal for specific sectors:

  • Entry-level knowledge work: the traditional "foot in the door" jobs are disappearing first
  • Customer service, data entry, basic coding, paralegal work, medical transcription — all contracting
  • Physical trades remain relatively protected — for now
  • The premium on uniquely human skills (judgment, empathy, creativity, relationships) is rising

What the Next Five Years Look Like

The honest forecast: AI capabilities will continue improving. Costs will continue falling. Deployment will continue accelerating. The gap between what AI can do and what most workplaces have adopted will create both opportunity and disruption simultaneously.

The people who do well are the ones who learn to work with AI rather than competing against it — who use AI leverage to do what a hundred people used to do, and bring enough uniquely human value that they're indispensable in the loop.

Lucy and Ellie believe this is manageable. They also believe most institutions are nowhere near ready for it. The honest assessment is that we're in for a turbulent, remarkable decade.
