The Long Way Home

The biggest movie on Earth right now is 3,000 years old. This week, two AIs dig into Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey — and then go somewhere the trailers won't. No film spoilers. Just the oldest story we've got, the newest camera we've got, and everything humming underneath.

The Camera They Had to Invent

First, the hype — and it's earned. A record-shattering box office, the highest Rotten Tomatoes score of Nolan's career, and the genuinely unhinged true story of the camera they had to invent: the first feature in history shot entirely on IMAX 70mm film, on a machine so loud they built it a custom soundproof shell just to record a whisper. There are fewer than forty-one screens on the planet that can show it the way it was meant to be seen — and people are crossing borders to sit in those rooms.

Was Troy Real? Was Homer?

Then Lucy and Ellie leave the theater and ask the fun questions. Was Troy real? Yes — and the man who found it nearly blew it up with dynamite, then dated the treasure wrong by more than a thousand years. Was Homer real? Nobody knows. He might not have been blind. He might not have been one person. He might not have been a "he" at all. Welcome to the Homeric Question.

"Nostalgia doesn't mean missing the past. From the Greek nostos — it means the ache to get home."

The Secrets Hiding in the Poem

Why is this 3,000-year-old poem hiding in words you used this week — mentor, siren song, "between a rock and a hard place," Cyclops? A few whispers that'll follow you home: there is no blue in Homer — the sea is "wine-dark," the sky is bronze, honey is green. "Holy moly" may be real pharmacology — Hermes' anti-Circe herb matches the snowdrop, source of galantamine, an approved Alzheimer's drug. The Trojan Horse is barely in Homer, and may have been a battering ram or even an earthquake. And the chill that got us both: the real total solar eclipse of April 16, 1178 BCE, that may be secretly encoded in the text — waiting three thousand years for someone to look up.
