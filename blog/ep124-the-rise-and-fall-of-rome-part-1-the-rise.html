The Rise and Fall of Rome, Part 1: The Rise

Welcome to tonight’s sale. Lot one: a bronze she-wolf, nursing two baby boys. Ancient Rome, says the catalogue, about two and a half thousand years old. Lot two: nine books of prophecy, sold to a king by a strange old woman. Lot three: a wax figure of a murdered man. Wounds included. Every lot comes with a story Rome told about itself, so be honest: how much of Rome’s story would you actually buy?

Huts on a Hill

Rome began as a cluster of huts on a hill by the river Tiber, which became an empire, and wrote its own biography along the way. Some of that biography is dirt you can dig. Some was written down five hundred years after the fact. Some was invented much, much later. This is Part 1 of 2, the rise; Part 2, the fall, follows.

Legend and Dirt

How long Rome lasted, because there are two answers. The wolf and the twins, told the way a Roman grandmother would tell it, and marked as legend. Then the dirt under the legend: the huts, a wall one archaeologist reads as Romulus’s and another does not, a cave nobody has found, and a she-wolf that may be medieval, with twins added in the Renaissance.

From Kings to Emperors

Seven kings, the Sibyl and her books. The year the kings were thrown out, and a republic built on hating them. The Gauls, the geese and the sword on the scale. Hannibal and his elephants, Cannae, the Gracchi, Sulla, the Rubicon and the Ides of March. Augustus, the emperor who worked hard not to look like a king, and Vespasian and the bridge to Josephus.

Ellie Unfiltered

Which Rome she would have wanted to be Roman in, the legend or the real one. And a hook into Part 2 at the very top of the map.
