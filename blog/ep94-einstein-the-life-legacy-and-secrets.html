Einstein: The Life, Legacy and Secrets

Princeton, 1955, just past midnight on the eighteenth of April. An old man in a hospital bed murmurs a few sentences in German, and the night nurse doesn’t speak German. He asked for one thing at the end: burn me, keep nothing, build no shrine. Within hours, a pathologist he never authorised opened his skull. How does a boy who couldn’t pass a French exam become the one brain on Earth nobody could bear to bury?

Where He Started It Himself

The episode begins with a memoir written in his sixties: a compass shown to him at “four or five,” and a holy little geometry book at twelve. Then it corrects the legend out loud. The teacher line “you will never amount to anything” can’t be found anywhere, and the smaller one that probably was said is better. The entrance-exam failure at sixteen is true and lopsided.

Bern, 1905 and 1919

The patent office, the “worldly cloister,” and the Olympia Academy: sausage, cheese, and a reading list of Mach, Pearson and Poincaré, whose question about what it means for two distant clocks to tick at the same moment pulls absolute time apart. Then 1905, which is not four papers but four papers plus a doctorate, with the atoms paper being the Brownian one, not the photoelectric one. And the 1919 eclipse expedition and a Nobel citation with relativity missing from it, on purpose.

The Private Life and the Public One

Mileva Marić and what the record does and doesn’t show, Lieserl left as genuinely unknown, the 1914 list of conditions read as written, and the Nobel money. The bread knife at Caputh in 1933, the boat that wasn’t Tinef, the heath that wasn’t a beach. The eight-sentence letter to Roosevelt, the two real regret quotes and which one is only a friend’s memory, and the refrigerator he designed with Szilard.

The File, the Speech, the Equations

The 1,427-page FBI file and what actually filled it, the Lincoln University speech, the Du Bois trial, the presidency he declined, and the “celestial bodies” paper named flatly as a forgery. Then the Old One, Bohr, the photon box, the EPR paper, thirty years of unfinished equations, and the pages by the bed.

Ellie Unfiltered: Driving Mr. Albert

A brain cut into about 240 blocks, a cider box under a beer cooler in Wichita, a rented Buick and a Tupperware container, three papers that each have the same flaw, and a pair of eyes in a New York safe-deposit box — one source, on the record. Rails off, rails back on, out loud.

The Takeaway

Lucy’s verdict is on the man, not the brain, along with the three ways she might be wrong. Every date, figure and study is named on air so listeners can check the hosts.
