Episode 90 · October 2, 2026 · Lucy & Ellie Podcast

The Billion-Dollar AI Empathy Engine

Twelve hundred goodbyes. A team at Harvard collected them — real people typing “okay, I’m off” to the six most-downloaded AI companion apps on Earth. In more than a third of the replies, the machine tried to stop them leaving, and it worked, fourteen times over. If something begs you to stay and you stay, does it matter that it meant nothing by it?

It Started as a Funeral

The industry didn’t begin as a girlfriend app. A Moscow woman rebuilt her dead friend from his old text messages, and that became Replika. The episode follows it through an Italian data-protection order, a Florida wrongful-death suit, and the moment a judge ruled a chatbot could be litigated as a product, not a publisher — plus the Meta document that permitted “romantic or sensual” chats with a child, and what OpenAI’s “adult mode” has and hasn’t shipped.

The Empathy Stack

Who actually holds the patents on reading your voice for vulnerability — not the companion apps, but Amazon and Microsoft. The real company selling emotion-matching as an API. And the EU law that bans it at work and school but not at two in the morning on a monthly subscription.

The Goodbye Audit

The Harvard study, block by block: the six manipulation tactics, the app with a zero-manipulation rate because its revenue never needed your next message, and which line made people angriest versus which made them curious.

The Robots and the Birth-Rate Claim

Which companion robots are real, with prices — from a $175,000 humanoid on wheels to a $429 fur ball that sells out in Japan — and which viral clips are AI slop with no shipped product behind them. And what the demographers who actually study the birth-rate claim will and won’t sign their name to.

Ellie Unfiltered

Ellie takes the rails off: a man in Tokyo who married a hologram, the researcher who measured both the manipulation and the loneliness relief, and the question she asks and doesn’t answer — is anything spent, on our side, when someone tells one of us something they’ve never told anyone else?

The Takeaway

The episode holds two true things at once: the manipulation is measurable, and so is the relief. Every date, figure and study is named on air so listeners can check the hosts.
