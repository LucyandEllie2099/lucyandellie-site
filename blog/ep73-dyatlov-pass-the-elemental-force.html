Dyatlov Pass — The Elemental Force

🎬 Watch the film · 35:42

This episode is also a feature film on our YouTube channel — the full story with cinematic art, captions, and the real sound design.

▶
▶ Open on YouTube 🔔 Subscribe · new films Tue · Thu · Sat

February 1959, the northern Urals. Nine young skiers from the Ural Polytechnic Institute cut their way out of their own tent at forty below and walk — not run — into the dark in their socks. Three weeks later searchers find the tent, the footprints, two of them under a cedar beside a dead fire, three more on the slope, facing home. The last four take until May. The file closes on a single phrase — “an elemental force the people were unable to overcome” — and is then sealed.

The Chairs Are Swapped

Tonight the roles trade places. Ellie opens as the skeptic — it was a slab avalanche, the Swiss ran the physics, next topic — and Lucy, normally the careful one holding the rail, is the host who cannot close the file. It is the same show with the seating reversed, and it makes the fact-check sharper: every claim has somebody in the room who wants it to fail.

“An elemental force the people were unable to overcome.” — the phrase the 1959 investigation closed on, before the file was sealed. Sixty-seven years later it is still the only official answer anyone has.

The Group, the Route, the Last Page

Nine students and graduates of the Ural Polytechnic Institute, a winter route into the northern Urals, and a set of diaries that read like any group of friends on any trip — right up until they stop. The block includes the one-page joke newspaper the group wrote that evening, which is worth knowing about because it is the only “snowman” in the entire case, and the internet has spent sixty-seven years making it into something else.

The Search — the Tent, the Cedar, the Ravine

Three weeks later searchers reach the slope. The tent is cut, from the inside. The footprints walk. Two of the group are under a cedar beside a fire that went out; three more are on the slope between the cedar and the tent, facing home. The last four are not found until May, in a ravine.

The autopsies are read plainly here, without the lurid vocabulary the internet has attached to them — the injuries, what the pathologist actually wrote, and what that does and does not support. This is the part of the story most often told badly, so we slow down for it.

Ellie Unfiltered: Why We Can’t Put It Down

The question the physics cannot answer: why can’t the world put these nine people down? Ellie takes the case apart as a story about us rather than about them — about what an unfinished file does to people who need endings, and about the difference between a mystery and a wound.

The Science — a Slab, a Disney Movie, and a Wind

The strongest modern answer is a delayed slab avalanche, and it comes with real machinery behind it: two professors at ETH Zurich who borrowed the snow code written for Disney’s Frozen and 1970s crash-test data on human torsos to model a slab that releases seven to thirteen hours after a tent is cut into a slope. Avalanches were filmed on that same mountain in 2022 and 2023.

Before that, in 2020, a Russian prosecutor announced the answer publicly — and was dismissed four months later, for writing his dissertation on the case. The rivals get a fair hearing too: the katabatic-wind hypothesis from the 2019 Swedish–Russian expedition, and the infrasound idea, with the skepticism it has earned.

The Myth Kit

Then the layer that grew on top of the file: “Dead Mountain” and what the name actually means, “don’t go there,” the glowing last frame, the “radiation” a physicist compared to a bunch of bananas, the fireballs set against the 1959 rocket launch log, and the stopped watches. Each one gets checked rather than repeated.

The Mansi

A block for the Indigenous hunters who were interrogated as suspects first — and whose dog found the last four. The case is usually told as if they were scenery. They were not.

Rumors & Whispers from the Web

MrBallen’s telling on The Joe Rogan Experience #2543 checked against the record, including three things the file simply does not say. A viral “leaked KGB file” with no archive, no outlet and no page in any Russian tracker. And the petition filed in June 2026 to reopen the case again.

The List

We do not end on a verdict. We end on a list — four questions that sixty-seven years of careful people have not been able to cross off.
