Not Just a Faster Computer

The most common misconception about quantum computing is that it's just a much faster version of a regular computer. It isn't. Quantum computers operate on fundamentally different principles — ones that shouldn't make intuitive sense, and yet produce real results that classical computers can't match.

Classical computers use bits — 0 or 1. Quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in superposition: 0, 1, or both simultaneously until measured. This isn't a metaphor. It's literal quantum mechanical behaviour that allows quantum computers to explore vast solution spaces in ways no classical machine can replicate.

"They operate on completely different rules — rules that shouldn't work, and yet do."

What Quantum Computers Can Actually Do

Quantum computers excel at specific types of problems. Optimization problems — finding the best route among billions of options. Simulation of molecular behaviour — critical for drug discovery. Breaking certain types of encryption. Modelling financial risk. These aren't hypothetical — quantum advantage has already been demonstrated in research settings.

The Encryption Problem

Perhaps the most consequential near-term application is cryptography. Much of the internet's security relies on the fact that factoring large numbers is computationally hard for classical computers. A sufficiently powerful quantum computer could break this encryption. Governments and cybersecurity researchers are already preparing for a post-quantum world.

  • Google claimed quantum supremacy in 2019
  • IBM, Google, and startups are racing to build fault-tolerant quantum systems
  • Post-quantum cryptography standards are already being developed
