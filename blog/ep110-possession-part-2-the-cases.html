Possession, Part 2 of 2: The Cases (Halloween)

Part 2 of 2, and the last night of the Halloween run. Last time the hosts read the rulebooks. Tonight the rulebook meets real people, oldest case first, each told as what the witnesses swore they saw against what the investigators found, and who was holding the story.

The Early Cases

Laon, 1566. Marthe Brossier in 1599 and the three tests set for her: water in a plain cup, a key wrapped in red silk, and a few lines of Virgil read like a prayer. Aix in 1611. Loudun in the 1630s, with a convent of Ursuline nuns, the parish priest Urbain Grandier, and the Jesuit Jean-Joseph Surin, who offered himself to be possessed as an act of atonement. Möttlingen, where the Lutheran pastor Johann Christoph Blumhardt gave us the words “Jesus is Victor.”

The Modern Cases

Pope Leo XIII and the prayer to St Michael, Padre Pio’s own letters beside Father Amorth’s telling, and two cases that each rest on one late witness: Clara Germana Cele in Natal in 1906 and Earling, Iowa, in 1928. The 1949 boy behind the famous film, whose name the hosts do not say. Anneliese Michel, 1952 to 1976, a documented illness, a death and a trial, told without a single joke.

Ellie Unfiltered

The demon who always agreed with the room, and the cases nobody could check. Faith-respectful throughout, with every claim named to its owner and legend told as legend.
