X-BAT: The Fighter with No Pilot and No Runway

Every fighter jet ever built needs a runway, wheels, a pilot and a radio. Shield AI, a defence company from San Diego, is building one with none of the four. It stands on its tail, goes straight up, and backs down onto its tail to come home.

What Exists Today

As recorded in mid-October, there is no flying X-BAT yet. The company says a first full-size prototype is weeks away and flight testing comes by the end of the year. The hosts read the asterisk on “the world’s first AI-piloted VTOL fighter jet,” meet a tail-sitter that rides a self-propelled cart, and the Ryan X-13 Vertijet of the 1950s, and hear one analyst’s worry that thirty-nine feet of wing could act like a sail in the wind.

The Pilot Nobody Can See

Hivemind is Shield AI’s software, and the company says it can fly without GPS or a radio link. The episode looks at its record, from the V-BAT drone to the X-62A VISTA test jet, which carried two safety pilots, and at a Reuters report on crashes in the company’s test fleet, told with Shield AI’s answer beside it.

Money and Race

Following the money, including why the four-hundred-million-dollar headline is half the company’s own, then the brochure numbers one by one, each marked as a company claim. The Navy’s runway-independent programme and a crowded race that includes Anduril’s Fury, General Atomics’ Dark Merlin, Australia’s Ghost Bat, Turkey’s Kizilelma and China’s GJ-11.

In the Loop, or on the Loop?

Under it all sits one question, read through the Defense Department’s Directive 3000.09 in its own words.
