Our Green Friends

The strangest intelligence on Earth has been sitting quietly in your window the whole time — and it doesn't have a brain.

A Mind With No Brain

We spend all our time wondering whether machines can think, or whether there's anyone out there in the dark. Meanwhile the oldest alien intelligence has been on the windowsill all along. Lucy and Ellie step into the green world and find something genuinely unsettling: plants that hear, signal, remember, cooperate, and scheme — with no nervous system, no face, and no heartbeat. This isn't a bag of fun plant facts. It's a mind we never learned to recognize.

It starts with a single bite. When a caterpillar breaks a leaf, that leaf sends a warning "telegram" to the rest of the plant — a chemical-electrical signal that travels at the speed of a sigh, and readies defenses the attacker hasn't reached yet.

The Wood-Wide Web

Beneath the forest floor runs a network older than trees themselves: a fungal internet linking roots across a whole woodland, trading sugar, water, and warnings. It has run for 450 million years with no outages. Along it, "mother trees" recognize and feed their own seedlings — while a strangler fig quietly wears the tree it killed.

"If intelligence doesn't need a brain, then we've been walking past minds our whole lives — and calling them scenery."

Schemers, Strangers, and the Question Underneath

Then it gets stranger. A flower spends ten years learning to smell like a corpse to lure its pollinators. A plant plays dead for years, waiting to keep an appointment with the rain. Each story tightens the same screw: if intelligence doesn't require a brain, what else have we been walking past without seeing? A quietly mind-bending episode about the neighbors we never introduced ourselves to.
