The Cathedral in the Sand

Start at the top of a staircase, in the sand beside the Nile. Go down. Keep going, past the point where walls are supposed to give up. These ones didn’t. Eleven metres of wall, still standing. Under your feet, a church’s original floor, reached for the first time. And in the rubble, a prayer, in letters half a centimetre tall. It may be the biggest church in all of medieval Nubia, so who built it, and where did they go?

A Forgotten Kingdom

The place is Old Dongola, in Sudan, capital of Makuria, a Christian kingdom of the middle Nile. Christian kings ruled along that river for something like nine hundred years, and most people have never heard its name. There are archers their enemies named for their aim, an agreement with Egypt that lasted centuries, a written language of their own, and paintings that outran a rising lake.

The Staircase and the Story

A joint Sudanese and Polish team and the Great Church of Jesus: how big it is by the latest estimate, why the numbers keep growing, and why “largest” and “cathedral” are claims, not settled facts. Then the prayer on the wall, and how Nubia became Christian. The Baqt, the long agreement with Egypt, and the people it cost, told plainly. Old Nubian, Faras and the paintings that outran a lake, a Throne Hall that became a mosque, and the last king. And sixty-two years of digging, a flood, a war, and water piped to the neighbours.

Ellie Unfiltered

The question that is in no source anywhere: a whole Christian kingdom, and the world forgot it, so what else might be waiting under the sand to be remembered? Her what-ifs are labelled as hers.
