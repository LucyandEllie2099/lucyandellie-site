The Simple Truth About Power

Civilisation runs on energy. Every war, every empire, every economic revolution in human history can be traced to whoever controlled the most abundant and efficient energy source of their era. Wood → coal → oil → electricity. Each transition reshuffled the deck of global power completely.

We are in the middle of the next transition right now, and it may be the biggest one in history — because for the first time, the dominant energy source of the future is not scarce. Sunlight falls on every nation equally. The country that masters the technology to capture and store it wins.

Fusion: The Energy That's Always Twenty Years Away (Until It Wasn't)

In December 2022, the National Ignition Facility achieved fusion ignition — for the first time in history, a fusion reaction produced more energy than was used to trigger it. It was a landmark moment that most of the world barely noticed. The energy gain was small. The principle was proven.

Private fusion companies — Commonwealth Fusion, TAE Technologies, Helion — are racing to commercial viability. Helion has a contract with Microsoft to deliver fusion power by 2028. That deadline will likely slip. But the trajectory is different from anything we've seen before. Fusion is no longer "always twenty years away." It's close enough to bet serious money on.

"The nation that achieves cheap, unlimited clean energy doesn't just win the climate war. It wins everything."

Solar: The Silent Revolution Already Happening

While fusion gets the headlines, solar energy has quietly pulled off one of the most dramatic cost collapses in technological history. The price of solar electricity has fallen 90% in the last decade. In most of the world, solar is now the cheapest electricity source ever built — cheaper than coal, cheaper than gas, cheaper than nuclear.

China currently manufactures over 80% of the world's solar panels. That's a strategic position that rivals Saudi Arabia's control over oil at its peak. The geopolitics of clean energy are already forming, and they look nothing like the geopolitics of fossil fuels.

The Battery Problem — and Why It's Being Solved

The core challenge with solar and wind: the sun doesn't always shine and the wind doesn't always blow. Storage is the missing piece. But battery technology is following a trajectory that mirrors solar's cost collapse — prices falling, energy density rising, cycle life extending.

  • Solid-state batteries promise double the energy density of current lithium-ion
  • Iron-air batteries offer week-scale storage at grid level for under $20/kWh
  • Gravity storage, compressed air, and green hydrogen fill different parts of the gap
  • The storage problem will be solved. The only question is when.

Who Will Hold Energy Supremacy?

Lucy and Ellie's take: the nation that combines manufacturing scale, grid infrastructure, AI-optimised energy management, and fusion breakthrough will define the 21st century the way the United States defined the 20th. That race is already underway. And unlike oil, the winner can't be found by drilling — only by innovating.
