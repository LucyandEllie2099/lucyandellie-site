The Real First Thanksgiving

Help me set the table, the way the picture always has it. A long wooden table. A golden turkey in the middle, cranberry sauce beside it, and a pumpkin pie cooling at the far end. Settlers down one side, their invited guests down the other. One autumn afternoon, a prayer, and then the word everyone’s waiting for: Thanksgiving. Now, a man who was actually there wrote it down, in a letter, that same year. So if he walked into our picture, how much of this table would he even recognise?

The Two Accounts

The place is Plymouth, on the coast of what is now Massachusetts, in the autumn of sixteen twenty-one. The letter-writer is an Englishman named Edward Winslow. And the people at the other end of that table were the Wampanoag. There are two English accounts and not one line from the Wampanoag side, so every dish and every guest gets held up against those two, with the weak pieces flagged.

We start with Winslow’s letter and what it does and does not say: the four men sent fowling, the ninety guests, the three days, the five deer. Then the menu, what the accounts name, what they don’t, and what is only our reasoning.

Patuxet and Tisquantum

The Mayflower’s first winter, and Patuxet, a Wampanoag village whose people were gone before the English arrived. Samoset, and then Tisquantum, the man you may know as Squanto: stolen across an ocean, home again to find his village empty, and caught between two powers.

The Treaty and the Name

The treaty, the name, and where the word “thanksgiving” really came from, including the rival first Thanksgivings in Florida and Texas. Then how one small colony’s harvest became a national holiday.

What Came After

What happened afterward to the people who sat at that table, told plainly. The Wampanoag voices still telling their own story today, a day of mourning, and a language being spoken again.

Ellie Unfiltered

Ellie asks what gratitude means when both sides of a table have lost so much, and her what-ifs are labelled as hers.

The Takeaway

The verdict, and the reasons the hosts might be wrong. Every word we have from those three days is English. Thank you for pulling up a chair at ours.
