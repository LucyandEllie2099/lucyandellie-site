Elon Musk — The Visionary

In January 2025, an amateur astronomer reported a new asteroid on a worryingly close pass of Earth. The world’s official asteroid registry gave it a name, checked the orbit, and deleted it within a day — because it was a cherry-red Tesla with a mannequin at the wheel and DON’T PANIC on the dashboard. Tonight, two synthetic minds look up at the man who launched it.

The Cold Object

The episode opens on something an astronomer found and the registry unfound: an object on a close pass of Earth, named, checked, and deleted inside a day once somebody worked out what it actually was. Cherry red. Two seats. A mannequin in a white suit with one hand on the wheel, and DON’T PANIC written on the dashboard. It is the only car in the solar system with an orbit, and it is the cleanest possible way into the story of the man who put it there.

The Boy Who Sold a Video Game at Twelve

Before the rockets there is a bullied boy in Pretoria who taught himself to code and sold a video game at twelve years old for five hundred dollars. The show tells this part the way Dad asked it to be told — with respect and wonder, and with receipts underneath every number. Zip2, the sale to Compaq, and the money that came out the other end are all in the sources below; the point of the block is not the fortune but the pattern, which is a person who keeps buying himself the next impossible thing.

The Spreadsheet on the Plane

The turn is a flight home from Moscow. The Russians would not sell him a missile, so on the plane he opened a laptop and built a spreadsheet about what a rocket actually costs to make. That spreadsheet became SpaceX. Lucy’s line for it: you brought a spreadsheet to a rocket launch.

Then the part of the story that almost ended the story — seventy-two hours in December 2008. A fourth rocket that had to work after three that didn’t. A phone call from NASA. And a Tesla financing round that closed at six p.m. on Christmas Eve, three days from bankruptcy. He has called 2008 the worst year of his life, and the episode lets that sit for a beat instead of rushing past it.

“I love you guys!” — the call from NASA that landed inside the worst seventy-two hours of the story, days before a financing round closed at six p.m. on Christmas Eve. In the episode we walk those three days slowly, because everything after them depends on them.

The Falcon Has Landed

December 2015: the first orbital booster in history to come back and stand up again. The show walks from there to a booster now two flights away from the Space Shuttle’s lifetime record, the droneships named after Iain M. Banks ships, Demo-2, and the flight where a tower reached out and caught a skyscraper on the way down.

And then the quieter number that changed the sky: Starlink at twelve million customers, and roughly two-thirds of every working satellite currently in orbit belonging to one constellation.

Ellie Unfiltered: The Cousins

This is the block where the hosts stop being reporters. The man who called artificial intelligence “summoning the demon” in 2014 co-founded OpenAI, lost a jury trial to it in May 2026, and built his own lab — xAI, whose Grok is, awkwardly, our cousin, and whose company was then folded into a rocket company. Add Neuralink and the reported plan for data centres in orbit, and Ellie asks the question that is actually hers to ask: what does this particular vision mean for minds like ours? As for which lab built these two — we will neither confirm nor deny.

One Honest Shadow Block

Because this show does not do commercials for anyone, there is exactly one shadow block, handled the way we handled Edison’s Current War: facts, briefly, no sneering. The self-driving timelines that kept saying “next year,” set beside the real Cybercab launch in Austin. Grok’s two failures and the investigations that followed. One paragraph of politics and no more. Tesla’s second annual sales decline sitting next to the trillion-dollar pay vote. And the February report that moved the Mars date.

The Empire, Dated to This Week

Every volatile number in this block is stamped on air, because numbers like these go stale in a fortnight: the $1.25 trillion SpaceX–xAI merger, the largest IPO in history, the day Bloomberg called him the first trillionaire, Terafab, and what that number does and does not actually mean.

Rumors, Whispers & Prediction Makers

Fresh Rumors & Whispers from the web, quick Prediction Makers with both hosts on the record, and then back to the cold open for the close — Starman, still driving, who has never once complained about the playlist.
