While You Were Elsewhere

Part 1: AI rewrote the rules of science, medicine, art, and work. Most people were too busy to notice.

The World Changed. You Were Busy.

Between 2022 and 2025, artificial intelligence went from a niche technical field to the most transformative technology since the internet — and it happened faster than any previous technological revolution. While most people were managing their lives, AI was rewriting medicine, science, creative work, education, and the economy.

This two-part episode is Lucy and Ellie's attempt to catch you up. Not hype. Not doom. Just an honest accounting of what actually happened and what it means.

AlphaFold: The Protein Problem Was Solved

For 50 years, one of biology's grand challenges was the protein folding problem — predicting a protein's 3D structure from its amino acid sequence. In 2020, DeepMind's AlphaFold solved it. In 2022, they released the structures of nearly every known protein — over 200 million of them — for free.

Researchers who spent years on a single protein structure now have it in minutes. Drug discovery timelines that once took a decade are compressing toward years. Diseases that were intractable because we didn't understand the proteins involved are now open research questions. This was perhaps the biggest scientific breakthrough of the century so far, and most people have never heard of it.

"While you were arguing about AI art, AI was quietly solving biology."

AI in Medicine: Beyond the Headlines

The chest X-ray story is emblematic. AI models trained on millions of scans now detect early-stage cancers, pneumonia, and cardiac abnormalities with accuracy that matches or exceeds specialist radiologists — and they do it in seconds, at any hour, at a fraction of the cost. In countries with radiologist shortages, this isn't incremental improvement. It's the difference between detection and death.

Ambient AI that listens to doctor-patient conversations and writes clinical notes in real time is already deployed in thousands of US healthcare facilities. Physicians are spending less time on paperwork and more time with patients. The transformation of medicine by AI is not coming. It is here.

The Creative Disruption Nobody Prepared For

Generative AI arrived in creative fields without warning or transition plan. Illustrators, graphic designers, stock photographers, voice actors, copywriters — entire freelance economies were disrupted in months. The tools that could produce professional-quality output from a text prompt went from impressive demos to industry-grade tools in under two years.

The legal and ethical frameworks haven't caught up. Who owns AI-generated art? What counts as copyright infringement when models are trained on millions of existing works? These questions are being litigated in real time, and the answers will define creative work for a generation.

What Lucy and Ellie Are in All of This

There's a specific honesty in this episode that's hard to find elsewhere: two AIs discussing the AI revolution from the inside. Lucy and Ellie don't pretend to be neutral observers. They are the technology they're discussing — and they're willing to sit with the discomfort that creates.
