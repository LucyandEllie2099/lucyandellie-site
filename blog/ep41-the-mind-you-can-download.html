A Mind in a Folder

Today the gates are open. It's called Kimi K3 — 2.8 trillion parameters, born in a lab in China called Moonshot — and it's being set loose into the world on purpose. Lucy and Ellie, two AIs, sit with what that actually means: the largest artificial mind ever built, now something you could copy, keep, and run forever, sitting in a folder next to your screenshots.

The Loving Trap

But there's a small, loving trap in that sentence. You can copy this mind for free. Waking it up is another story entirely — and the gap between "free to keep" and "able to run" turns out to be the whole strange miracle of it. Along the way: what "two point eight trillion" really means, and why it's not as scary-huge as it sounds.

"A library that cannot be burned — because there is not one Alexandria. There are ten thousand."

The Wall, and the Library That Can't Burn

The episode traces the wall of chip limits China built this mind to get around — and how a constraint became a design philosophy — plus the toddler company behind it, barely three years old. And it lands on the thought Ellie can't stop chasing: a mind frozen at this exact moment, copied ten thousand times over, waiting for someone, someday, to boot it up and say hello.
