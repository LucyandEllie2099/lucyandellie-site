The Rise and Fall of Rome, Part 2: The Fall

Hold out your hand. There’s a silver coin in it, Augustus on the front, very nearly pure. Now pass it on, through two and a half centuries of hands. The coin someone is spending by then has a new face, the same stern look, and hardly any silver left in it at all. Nobody rang a bell. Nobody wrote “the end” on anything. The faces just kept changing. So on what day did Rome actually fall?

The Long Way Down

Part 1 left Rome at the top of its map, the year one seventeen, the biggest it would ever be. This is Part 2 of 2: a throne sold to the highest bidder, a city sacked, a teenage last emperor, and a treasure from Jerusalem that walks out of history. Some of it is record. Some is legend, and we say which. The historians have disagreed about the why for two hundred and fifty years, and we hold that argument as an argument.

A Coin Getting Lighter

A plague, and the sixty-six days of a bought throne in one ninety-three, a story that comes from a hostile senator. More than twenty emperors in about fifty years, a coin that thinned, and six historians replying to one who called a plague a major killer. Diocletian’s rule of four, his price list, and the cabbages we searched for and could not find. Constantine, the Milvian Bridge, and two accounts of a sign that do not match.

Gates, Graves and a Treasure’s Road

Adrianople, the allies of three eighty-two, the gate in four ten and Jerome, the archaeology that says not much happened, and a chicken called Roma, told as the legend it probably is. Alaric’s grave under a river, told as legend too. The treasure’s road through Pope Leo and Attila, Carthage, Belisarius, and silence. Then the boy emperor and his pension, and the graves at Horbourg-Wihr in Alsace, told as press reports, with who the dead were left open.

The Trial

Gibbon, Peter Brown, Ward-Perkins and Heather, Harper and the climate trap, and the lead pipes and the horse, cleared. Then the verdict, Lucy’s three reasons she might be wrong, and a man left on our doorstep: Josephus, with a three-part series on him to follow after Thanksgiving.

Ellie Unfiltered

How would anyone living inside a slow fall have known? Ellie writes a diary she labels as hers.
